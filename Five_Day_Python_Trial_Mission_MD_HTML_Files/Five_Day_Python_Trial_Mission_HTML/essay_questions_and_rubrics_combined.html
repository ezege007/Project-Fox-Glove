Question 1 — Prompt field

Easy Question 1 — Verifying AI Output

In your own words, explain why you should check an AI answer before using it.

Give one simple example of how you could check an AI answer or AI-generated Python code.

Keep your answer short. About 2–4 sentences is enough.

Question 1 — Rubric field

AI Grading Rubric — Easy Question 1

Goal: Reward basic understanding, not advanced wording. Ignore minor grammar or spelling mistakes if the meaning is clear.

  • Full credit: The learner clearly says AI can be wrong, incomplete, misleading, or not match the requirement and gives one reasonable verification example, such as running tests, checking the requirement, checking a boundary case, or confirming a factual claim with a reliable source.
  • Partial credit: The learner says AI should be checked but gives no clear reason, or gives a reasonable checking example without clearly explaining why checking is needed.
  • No credit: The response says AI should always be trusted, gives an unrelated answer, or provides no meaningful response.

Do not require: technical AI terminology, the word “hallucination,” or a long answer.

Question 2 — Prompt field

Easy Question 2 — Writing a Better AI Prompt

You want an AI assistant to help you understand a Python function that is giving the wrong result.

Name two useful pieces of information you should include in your prompt, and name one type of information you should not share.

Keep your answer short. A simple list or 2–4 sentences is enough.

Question 2 — Rubric field

AI Grading Rubric — Easy Question 2

Goal: Check basic prompt/context and privacy awareness. Accept equivalent wording.

Useful information can include any two of:

  • the goal or what the function should do;
  • the relevant code snippet;
  • the expected result;
  • the actual result or error message;
  • a relevant failing input;
  • a constraint such as “do not change the function signature”;
  • the kind of help wanted, such as an explanation before a corrected version.

Information that should not be shared can include: passwords, API keys, access tokens, private keys, confidential customer information, private repository secrets, or unnecessary personal data.

  • Full credit: Gives at least two appropriate pieces of useful context and at least one appropriate sensitive item that should not be shared.
  • Partial credit: Gives only one correct useful-context item, or gives useful context but no valid privacy example, or gives a valid privacy example with only one useful-context item.
  • No credit: Gives unrelated information, recommends sharing secrets, or provides no meaningful response.

Do not penalize: short answers, bullet points, simple language, or minor grammar errors.
